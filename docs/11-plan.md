# FlexTriage Origami Bay project plan

The project is at TRL 3 on paper (stage P3). The next gate is the team's own: evidence that two people deploy a functional three-zone bay in under 15 minutes with no training. At TRL 4 that means a timed trial with untrained people (V1), with the stability push (V2) and the sheet's fire report (V5) alongside. P4 work is on hold under the portfolio cap and is listed here as planned work only.

## Purpose

Give emergency medical teams, outbreak coordinators and field hospital operators a bay they can stand up in minutes that turns an open floor into intake, assessment and treatment zones with a one-way flow and cues anyone can read, supporting their triage and IPC practice without permanent construction.

## Kill criteria (project stop rules, not a product claim)

From FXT-PRB-001: stop or change course if, at TRL 4, two untrained people cannot deploy a functional three-zone bay in under 30 minutes after the fixes; a wall tips at a stub joint under the 178 N push with the specified ballast; the bought sheet fails NFPA 701 Test Method 1; printed cues are misread by more than one in ten users; or field partners judge a bay of about 40 kg in four bags unusable.

## Targets and key performance indicators

| KPI | Target | Now (paper) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Deployment, two untrained people | Under 15 min | 12 to 35 min estimate with the deployment kit |
| Pack mass | 12 kg in two bags (team); D2 and platform D15 | 43.1 kg in three bags of about 15 kg (35 kg with moulded, cored parts) |
| Push at a stub joint, 178 N at 1,110 mm | No tip, no slide | 312 N tipping; 177 to 236 N sliding |
| Push between stubs | Criterion to set (D7) | 5 to 15 N |
| Doorway clear width | At least 815 mm | 1,054 to 1,069 mm |
| Leaf opening force | At most 22.2 N | About 15.3 N |
| Reuse | 50 deployments | To verify |
| Cost per bay | USD 800 (stretch under USD 500) | USD 1,174.23 |
| Cost per deployment over 50 | Track | USD 23.48 |

## Minimum viable team

A mechanical lead (sheet work and printed joints), a test lead with access to a force gauge and a fire test report route, a field liaison with an emergency medical team, an IPC adviser for the cleaning and procedure review, and a logistics volunteer. Helpful Engineering volunteers can fill each role; names are not recorded here until Amish assigns them.

## 60 to 90 day plan once P4 opens

1. Days 1 to 15: Amish decides D1, D2, D7 and D10; buy sheet with its NFPA 701 report; set up a print pool.
2. Days 15 to 45: build one bay to FXT-BLD-001; clip pull-out and cycle tests on prints (T2, T3); catch force (V3).
3. Days 45 to 75: timed deployments with three pairs of untrained people (V1, the decision gate); push tests at and between stubs (V2); wipe test (V4).
4. Days 75 to 90: walk-through with an emergency medical team and an IPC adviser; report against the decision gate and the project stop rules.

## Support needed from Helpful Engineering

Mechanical and materials support for the printed clips and the creep question, introductions to emergency response teams (WHO EMT, MSF) and FEMA for co-design and viability input, an IPC adviser, and a materials test partner for structural assessment of the panels, as the team's project update asks.

## External partners to explore

Emergency medical teams for co-design and trials; a materials testing laboratory (clip pull-out, cycles, creep); an FR twinwall supplier with NFPA 701 reports; makerspaces or print farms; funders interested in rural and surge health capacity (the team deck cites the US rural health fund).

## Next steps and decisions

Decision request to Amish: D1 (FT1, FT3 or FT2, all within 10 %), D2 (pack target), D7 (lateral criterion) and D10 (clip holding, with platform D14 and D15), then D3, D4, D6, D8 and D9 (FXT-DEC-001). No further design work until D1 is decided.
