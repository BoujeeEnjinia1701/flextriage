# FlexTriage Origami Bay design precis

The bay turns 18 pre-scored panels of flame-retardant polypropylene twinwall into a serpentine three-zone triage space: entry, 1 intake, 2 assessment, 3 treatment, exit, one way. It is built on the shared Flex platform (FXP-PLT-001): P12 duo panels (1,200 x 1,200 mm, one fold) stand folded 30 deg in zigzag walls, in feet on rubber pads, and join with tool-free clip-lock angle clips; printed numerals, words and arrows mark the zones; one leaf on two pin hinges closes each doorway on a magnetic catch. It encloses 19.2 m2, needs about 8.8 x 2.9 m of floor, weighs about 43.1 kg with its hardware and bags, and its parts cost USD 1,174.23 against the USD 800 value-engineering target (FXT-CAL-001). It supports IPC practice by giving cohorts physical boundaries and a one-way path; it makes no claim on transmission and is not a barrier. The choice between FT1 and the alternatives (fabric screens FT2, floor marking and stanchions FT3) is **proposed, awaiting Amish**: all three score within 10 % (FXT-OPT-001, FXT-DEC-001 D1). CONCEPT, NOT FOR FABRICATION.

## How it works

Two long walls of seven P12 duos run 8.19 m from end to end, 2.34 m apart on their joint lines. Every wall is a zigzag, as the platform's v0.2 rule S1 asks: each duo is folded 30 deg at its groove and each wall joint is an angle clip turning 30 deg the other way, so every leaf stands 15 deg off the wall line, held on both vertical edges, and the fold vertices point out of the bay (F10). Four zigzag stubs, each one duo, stand out from the walls at alternate sides (the left end and divider 2 from wall A, divider 1 and the right end from wall B), so each leaves a doorway against the opposite wall and the path snakes through the zones. Every joint is clipped at two heights (150 and 1,050 mm above the panel's foot); an angle tee takes each stub; at the two end-stub corners a 600 mm tail leaf outside the bay turns the corner into a tee (F7). Each stub ends in an anchor foot that pegs its lower holes, carrying two 5 kg site-filled sand bags, so a wall pushed outward next to a stub has to lift the stub, its foot and its bags: 312 N against the platform's 178 N push (F4). Away from the stubs the walls are boundaries, not barriers: a lean tips them (FXT-STM-001 S6, D7).

Each doorway has one 1,149 x 990 mm leaf hung on two 6 mm stainless pins at the stub end. It opens downstream only, with about 15 N on its push plate, and closes against a stop clip at the wall jamb, where an N42 disc behind 2 mm pulls a stainless strike on the leaf (F3). EPDM finger guards cover the hinge gap (F9).

## Changes from the team concept (iteration 2)

- The team's concept is option FT1 and is carried; its working principle is kept. Plan, hardware and pack details changed under the fix rule (F1 to F10, FXT-DEC-001), and three claims go to Amish: the 12 kg pack (D2), the floor-space statement (D4) and the lateral stability criterion away from the stubs (D7). The platform resolved the fourth (rules S1 and S4 for straight runs) with zigzag walls, which this design now uses.
- Wall height: the deck gives none; FT1 uses the 1.2 m P12 module (boundary and staff line of sight). The 1.8 m P18 variant would add privacy at 53.7 kg; as a zigzag it now stands under its own weight (D3).
- "PVC-lock joints" are read as the platform clip-lock family, with no PVC (FXP-PLT-001 D13).
- "Magnetic flap openings" are realised as a single-acting leaf with a pull-mode magnetic catch.
- The runner-up's best idea is grafted on: FT3's floor tape marks the path and the 1 m spacing inside the zones (a printed floor marking kit is proposed in D1 as an add-on).

## First-order numbers (FXT-CAL-001)

- Zones 5.48 (intake), 5.48 (assessment) and 8.22 m2 (treatment); interior 19.2 m2; floor 8.8 x 2.9 m plus 1.5 m approach at each end.
- Mass 43.1 kg with bags: sheet 22.3 kg, hardware 19.4 kg; four bags of about 11 kg or three of about 15 kg (platform D15); about 35 kg with moulded, cored feet and clips.
- Stability: 312 N at a stub joint with two bags per anchored stub; about 5 to 15 N between stubs (boundary only).
- Doorways 1,054 to 1,069 mm clear at the floor; leaf push about 15 N; catch about 13 N.
- Deployment by two people 12 to 35 min (estimate, with F6); one zone 1.6 to 4.7 min.
- Fire load about 1,279 MJ (sheet 887 MJ); FR grade mandatory.
- Self-weight: each held leaf 9.4 cold, 4.7 hot; a leaf with one free edge about 4.7 and 2.3 (the flat v0.1 walls were 0.87 hot).
- Parts USD 1,174.23 (target USD 800); about 672 printer-hours of PETG for the prototype.

## Safety

> **Safety:** The bay is a boundary, not a barrier: its walls tip if leant on or struck away from the stubs, so nobody leans, sits or hangs anything on them, and trolleys are kept clear of the walls. Use it only indoors or inside a tent, standing on a level floor, with every anchor and jamb foot ballasted as the build plan shows. Keep it 1 m from heaters, flames and oxygen outlets, and off the host tent's or hall's exit routes. The leaves open one way; return by pulling. The catch magnets carry a pacemaker label (keep 10 cm away). Use only sheet bought with an NFPA 701 Test Method 1 report.

## Release limitation

No prototype is used in patient care until its stability, fire performance, cleanability and the procedure it embeds are reviewed by qualified IPC and safety staff (STANDARDS 25.5b; FXT-CLM-001).
