# FlexTriage Origami Bay feasibility assessment

The origami panel bay is buildable on paper from one sheet grade, prints and a few bought parts, and its zones, cues and doorways work. It is not feasible as the deck states it: it weighs 43.1 kg against 12 kg, costs USD 1,174 against USD 800, and its walls stand a bump only next to the stubs. Those three findings are for Amish (D2, D7, value engineering).

Table 1. Feasibility by aspect.

| Aspect | Assessment | Rating | Risks and notes | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Design | Serpentine three-zone plan of zigzag walls and stubs from 18 duos, 4 leaves and 2 tails; 181 parts at D3 with no interference | High | Meets the platform v0.2 rules S1 and S4; the angle clip needs a round core (platform request 8) | FXT-OPT-001; `cad/fidelity.json` |
| Performance: stability | 312 N at a stub joint with two bags per anchor; 5 to 15 N between stubs | Low | The deck's "stable" holds only near stubs; a stated limit (D7) | FXT-STM-001 S5, S6 |
| Performance: zones and cues | 3 zones of 5.5, 5.5 and 8.2 m2; neutral numeral and word signs legible at 9 to 18 m | High | Sight over 1.2 m walls hides a floor stretcher within 2.3 m | FXT-CAL-001 sections 2, 9 |
| Performance: doorways | 1,054 to 1,069 mm clear; leaf holds at 12.9 N and opens at 15.3 N on paper | Medium | Magnet and strike grade to confirm (C6, C7) | FXT-STM-001 S11, S12 |
| Materials | FR PP twinwall at most 700 g/m2; PETG prints; EPDM; stainless pins | Medium | Prints are not FR; folds and leaves hold their self-weight hot at 4.7 (A15) | FXT-STM-001 S14, S38 |
